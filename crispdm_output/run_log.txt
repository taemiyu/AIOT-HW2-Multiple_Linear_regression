PHASE 1: BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING
======================================================================
objective: Help health policy analysts estimate a country's life expectancy from health, economic and social indicators, and identify which indicators matter most.
business_questions: ['Which indicators are most strongly associated with life expectancy?', 'How accurately can life expectancy be predicted for a country the model has never seen?', 'How uncertain is a single prediction (prediction interval)?']
data_mining_goal: Multiple linear regression: Life expectancy = f(indicators), with feature selection, evaluated on unseen countries.
success_criteria: {'test_R2_min': 0.8, 'test_RMSE_max': 4.5, 'cv_R2_min': 0.75}

--- 1.2 Situation assessment ---
resources: One public Kaggle CSV (2,938 rows, 22 columns), Python with scikit-learn and statsmodels.
assumptions: Indicators are comparable across countries and years.
constraints: The model must stay interpretable (linear) for policy discussion.
risks: ['Many missing values (44% of rows have at least one).', 'Implausible values (e.g. average BMI above 50) indicate data-entry errors.', 'Each country appears up to 16 times; a random split would leak the same country into training and testing.']
cost_benefit: Cheap to build; useful for prioritising health spending, but associations are not causal effects.

--- 1.4 Project plan ---
 - Phase 2: profile data, missing values, implausible values, correlations
 - Phase 3: clean, log-transform skewed variables, split by country, impute (same-country median, then training median)
 - Phase 4: VIF, feature selection (Lasso, Forward SFS, RFE, ElasticNet), OLS / Ridge / Lasso
 - Phase 5: test metrics vs success criteria, assumption checks, prediction intervals
 - Phase 6: deploy only if approved; save model and report
